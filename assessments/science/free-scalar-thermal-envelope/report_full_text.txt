   1 Executive assessment
   Herzog and Stergiou propose that a free scalar bounds the thermal entropy-to-stress-tensor ratio of every
   unitary three-dimensional conformal field theory (CFT). They also propose a stronger hyperbolic
   heat-capacity envelope. They present a conditional result near the conformal temperature and test
   selected examples; they do not claim a universal proof [1].

   The audit reproduced the free-field ratios, selected thermal-response values, two planar Chern–Simons
   benchmarks, finite-N input combinations and several next-to-leading coefficients. A second pass
   reproduced the recorded numerical output byte-for-byte on the same runtime and narrowed the
   interpretation of those checks. The sampled calculations use the paper’s spectral densities and saddle
   equations; replay is not a validation of every physical premise.

   A subsequent phase-transition search found no qualifying counterexample. A constant-auxiliary-field
   ordered solution is excluded on the checked reference branch under normalizability assumptions. A
   coupled nonconstant branch has not been solved or excluded. The final finite-disk work calibrated only one
   radial Laplacian sector; it did not implement the interacting Green function, determinant, nonlinear saddle
   search, or free-energy comparison.

   2 Scientific checks
   In the paper’s conventions, s(T) = cS T² and the separated stress-tensor two-point function is normalized
   by CT. The conformal temperature is T0 = 1/(2πR), x = T/T0, and Ψ = 12R² sH/(π³ CT), where sH is
   hyperbolic entropy density [1, Eqs. (1), (2), (9)–(13)].

   Free fields
   For one real scalar, the flat-space thermal free energy is f = −ζ(3) T³/(2π), so cS = 3ζ(3)/(2π). A
   separated-point Wick calculation gives CT = 3/(32π²). Their ratio is 16πζ(3) = 60.42197018. For one
   two-component complex Dirac fermion, the entropy integral and cited CT convention give 12πζ(3) =
   45.31647763 [1, Eqs. (3)–(4); 3]. These are free-field checks, not a proof for interacting CFTs.

   Conditional local argument
   Using the paper’s twist-operator inputs and thermodynamic identities gives Ψ″(1) = 3/4 + t4/240. For
   theories to which the boundary-free formula applies, the collider constraint gives 11/15 ≤ Ψ″(1) ≤ 23/30.
   The conformal scalar requires a modular-Hamiltonian boundary term. The cited correction δh″1 = −π/512
   gives δΨ″scalar(1) = 1/2 and Ψ″scalar(1) = 19/15 [1, Eqs. (16)–(24); 4, 5].

   For each covered theory, right differentiability and the stated identities give D(1+δ) = aδ + o(δ), where D
   = Ψ′scalar − Ψ′ and a ≥ 1/2. Therefore D is positive in a sufficiently small, potentially theory-dependent
   right neighborhood. A uniform neighborhood is not established. The integrated parity-odd/contact-term
   treatment was not independently completed.

   3 Numerical evidence and corrections
   Selected endpoint checks
   Using the paper’s thermal masses and entropy integrals, the critical O(N) and Bardeen–Moshe–Bander
   leading-large-N entropy fractions reproduce 4/5 and 7/8 of the free-scalar value, respectively. For the
   planar regular-boson Chern–Simons saddle at λ = 1/2, the two reported ratios recompute to 49.0337677
   and 48.8231125 [1, Eqs. (6), (34), (36), (S43)–(S54)]. These checks assume the physical saddle and cited
   input equations.

   The finite-N Ising and O(N) endpoint rows were recombined from published Casimir and stress-tensor
   inputs. The O(4) and O(6) rows use stress-tensor lower-bound inputs and are upper estimates. The audit
   also recombined published coefficients to reproduce the paper’s displayed next-to-leading O(N),
   Gross–Neveu and QED3 expansions. It did not independently redo the source simulations or diagrammatic
   computations.

   Audit of the audit
   PASS labels: numerical evaluation of authors’ formulas, finite differences sharing a solver, and
   same-runtime replay establish the specific calculations only. They are not independent validation of the
   model, boundary conditions, global phase selection, or the universal conjectures.

   Local remainder: the positive leading margin applies for each covered theory in a potentially
   theory-dependent neighborhood. A written O((x−1)²) term requires suitable remainder control; no uniform
   neighborhood is established.

   Ising: the chosen Monte Carlo amplitude −0.1531(4) and CT/CT,scalar = 0.946538675(42) yield about
   51.08. The Monte Carlo paper also reports a thermal-bootstrap-derived comparison −0.1425(15). The
   latter’s conversion, attributed to private communication, was not independently reconstructed. Using the
   same CT gives about 47.55; both central values are below the conjectured 60.42 bound. The discrepancy
   is an evidence-quality qualification, not a counterexample [6, 7].

   Topological sectors: the handling of global or boundary contributions in the regulated hyperbolic
   entropy merits an explicit calculation. No inconsistency in Eq. (11) was established; this was downgraded
   to an unresolved question.

   4 Competing phases and saddle search
   A first-order transition at xc > 1 with positive entropy-density jump ΔsH would introduce a distributional
   contribution to the normalized response: Ψ′ ⊃ [12R² ΔsH/(π³ CT)] δ(x−xc). If the scalar response is finite
   there and the jump survives the relevant normalization and limits, that would challenge the pointwise
   envelope. A qualifying unitary theory, replica boundary conditions, physical dominant phases and nonzero
   jump must all be established.

   The literature search did not identify such a case. The earlier critical O(N) analysis reports an instability at
   or near the conformal point, with the studied instability on the low-temperature side. It did not determine a
   nonconstant competing saddle’s free energy, and could not completely exclude a higher-temperature
   first-order transition. Holographic low-temperature transitions and non-normalizable-mode examples are
   not qualifying high-temperature vacuum-Rényi counterexamples without additional checks [2, 8].

   Constant auxiliary field
   On the checked symmetric x > 1 branch, the fundamental fluctuation operator is K = −∂τ² − ΔH² − 1/(4R²)
   + m²/R². For normalizable fields, −ΔH² ≥ 1/(4R²), and m > 0. Therefore ⟨φ,Kφ⟩ ≥ (m²/R²)||φ||², excluding a
   nonzero normalizable solution of Kφ = 0 with that constant auxiliary field. This says neither that the
   complete coupled Hessian is positive nor that a disconnected nonconstant saddle cannot dominate.

   A physical competing branch would require solving the coupled equations Kσ φ = 0 and φ² − σ + Gσ(z,z) =
   0 with common regulator, counterterms, contour and replica boundary conditions. No such admissible
   branch was found or constructed here. No ΔF, entropy jump or transition temperature was calculated.

   The strict N → ∞ limit, hyperbolic-volume limit, regulator removal and finite-N limit are distinct. Finite N
   does not by itself rule out a thermodynamic transition. A future intermediate-temperature failure of the
   hyperbolic conjecture would not automatically disprove the separate flat-space bound.

   5 Radial calibration
   A finite hyperbolic disk with radius parameter L, R = 1, regular origin and Dirichlet outer boundary was
   used to calibrate the static, rotationally symmetric scalar Laplacian. A cell-centered finite-volume
   discretization was compared against an independent ODE shooting calculation. The Dirichlet disk is a
   chosen regulator; its full relation to the vacuum Rényi boundary prescription was not established.

   Grid refinement improves agreement for this finite eigenproblem. At L = 9, λ0 − 1/4 ≈ 0.094821; at x =
   1.1 the infinite-volume reference mass satisfies m² ≈ 0.000589. Thus the chosen cutoff’s spectral shift is
   much larger than that mass term near x = 1. This is a regulator limitation, not a continuum instability.

   Not completed: the finite-disk angular and Matsubara sums, spatially varying interacting Green function,
   determinant, common counterterms and contour, nonlinear searches from multiple initial profiles,
   candidate stability checks, and free-energy comparisons. The infinite-volume gap and entropy replay
   cannot substitute for finite-regulator calibration of those components.

   Next discriminating calculation
   Implement a common finite-disk Green function and determinant, verify convergence to the symmetric
   gap and entropy at x = 1.1 and 2, then search admissible nonconstant profiles and compare consistently
   renormalized free energies. A finite-cutoff stationary point must not be labeled a continuum saddle or
   dominant phase without the required checks.
